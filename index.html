SIMLENS: Sound-Informed Multimodal LLMs for Full-Curve Video Engagement Prediction

WACV 2027

SIMLENS predicts a video's per-second Most Replayed curve from its frames and soundtrack, including the moments viewers skip, before the video is published.

Abstract

YouTube's Most Replayed curve captures relative replay intensity across a video, but becomes available only after publication. We introduce SIMLENS to predict this curve in advance, capturing both frequently replayed moments and low-replay regions. SIMLENS adapts a pretrained audio-visual large language model by grouping visual and audio tokens into one-second blocks and appending per-second queries after the complete content sequence, allowing every prediction to use full-video context. A bidirectional listwise objective learns per-second rankings from both the high- and low-replay ends of the curve. On Mr.HiSum, SIMLENS reaches 77.5 mAP@50 and a per-video Spearman correlation of 0.527 while adapting only 0.11% of the model parameters. To the best of our knowledge, SIMLENS achieves state-of-the-art performance on this benchmark, with an 11.1-point mAP@50 improvement over the strongest baseline evaluated on the same test subset. Controlled ablations evaluate the contributions of audio, suffix-query readout, and listwise supervision. The same checkpoints generalize without additional tuning to YTMR500 and our new YT-Fresh benchmark of 1,230 recent videos. These results demonstrate the potential of audio-visual models to anticipate replay patterns and provide creators with feedback before publication.

Method

Frozen visual and audio encoders produce tokens that are interleaved in one-second blocks. The decoder of Qwen2.5-Omni, adapted with LoRA, reads the complete video followed by one suffix query per second, so every query can use the whole video, and a linear head maps each query to a replay score. A bidirectional listwise loss learns the ordering of seconds from both the most-replayed and the least-replayed end of the curve, and a first-difference term preserves local changes. Only 0.11% of the parameters are trained, and the full curve comes out of a single forward pass.
